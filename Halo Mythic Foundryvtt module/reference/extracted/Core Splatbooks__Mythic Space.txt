MYTHIC SPACE 
Mythic Space introduces space -faring vessels to introduce Halo’s 
space combat into Mythic. Thanks to a partnership with Sins of the 
Prophets, I was allowed to begin this project using the renders and 
gain inspiration from their game. 
 For use with the Mythic 6.3 or newer handbook. 
 
INTRODUCTION 
Mythic Space brings in the mighty warships of the Halo universe, 
introducing massive celestial ships designed to dish out as much 
destruction as possible. The GM must be aware that Mythic’s 
Warships are on an entirely different level from Vehicles and 
Characters. Their Characteristics are scaled differently, with most 
instances of Ship vs. Vehicle ending in instant destruction of the 
vehicle. These are not vessels to be taken lightly. 
 
PUTTING BUTTS IN SEATS 
In Halo, ship combat has many levels to it. On the smallest level of 
personal transportation ships, it only takes one or two individuals to 
safely pilot the ship. When it comes to Warships, it takes dozens, if 
not thousands to safely run a ship. Even just the simple course of 
piloting a ship and maintaining trajectory can take  several people to 
perform. But that’s not fun for the Players. So you as a party have two 
options. You can either have the Players control certain aspects of a 
ship, or you can have characters being active on a ship for roleplaying 
and even combat scenarios. 
 
THE SIZE OF A WARSHIP 
Warships in the Halo universe are hundreds of meters at their 
smallest, well above several kilometers at their largest. The amount 
of personnel it takes to keep a ship running safely is also larger than 
some towns. This means that even when not personally piloting or 
controlling an aspect of a Ship, the Players will have a lot they could 
do on a ship of t his size. Readying troops, preparing for battle, 
roleplaying scenarios out, fighting off intruders, and so much more. 
 
PREMADE SHIPS 
Mythic Space will come with dozens of premade Ships for both the 
UNSC and the Covenant. These will allow for quick and easy access to 
Ships based on price and capability, as well as acting as a Bestiary. All 
Premade Ships will come with a Price that must be paid to use. 
 
SHIP BUILDING 
Ship Building allows for Players and the GM to build their own Ships 
using Frames. The Frames that are available are split between 
Warships and Civilian Frames. Warship Frames are based on existing 
Warship classifications from the Halo Universe, allowing y ou to build 
an entirely new type of Warship from scratch without needing to just 
modify an existing premade Ship.

When running games, you should be using very large grids sizes to 
better track positions and directions on a grand scale. The following 
is a table that has recommended grid sizes for combat and map 
scenarios.

SHIP FACINGS 
Ship facings are the originations of a vessel both on water and in space 
relative to its surroundings. These terms are essential for navigation, 
communication, and understanding attack directions. The following 
are the Base Facings of Ship Combat. 
 
FORE: The direction pointing towards the front of the Ship. 
AFT: The direction pointing behind the back of the Ship. 
PORT: The direction pointing left of the Ship. 
STARBOARD: The direction pointing right of the Ship. 
 
BOW: The front section of the Ship. 
STERN: The back section of the Ship. 
MIDSHIP: The middle section of the Ship. 
 
 
 
SHIP CHARACTERISTICS 
Ship Characteristics are similar to Vehicle Characteristics. The 
following will explain each Ship Characteristic and how it is used in 
combat. 
 
COST 
Ships will list a price needed to purchase them. The GM may discount 
or give Ships for free depending on the mission and type of game 
being played. A Ship’s cR cost is substantially higher than anything 
else in all of Mythic, so the GM must take this into account that most 
games would never allow for a ship to be afforded. 
 
SHIP TYPE 
Ship Types are broken down into groupings that generalize the level 
of strength the Ship will have. These Types are broken up into the 
following categories, listed in order of smallest and weakest to biggest 
and strongest on average: 
• Corvette 
• Frigate 
• Destroyer 
• Cruiser 
• Carrier 
• Battleship 
• Supercarrier

MANPOWER 
Manpower is the amount of workers needed to keep a ship running 
at its full potential. When a Ship takes Special Damage, there is a 
chance of reducing Manpower of a Ship. When Manpower becomes 
too low, a Ship’s Characteristics and available Actions can be reduced. 
The following table lists what happens when Manpower drops to 
certain percentages of its original count. When Manpower drops 
below a percentage, it gains the negative outcomes of it and all 
percentages above. Meaning if a Ship drops below 50% Manpower, it 
takes the Penalties for 50% and 75%. 
 If the Player Characters would be killed due to Manpower 
being Damaged by a Weapon or Special Damage, the Player 
Characters should be allowed to use their Luck to survive as they 
would normally. The GM should only ever target the Players with 
Manpower Damage when they are the very last possible Characters 
to be affected. 
 
MANPOWER OUTCOME 
75% -10 to all To Hit Tests. 
60% Reload Times increased by 1 for all weapons. 
45% Maneuver and Tactics Tests gain -20 Penalty. 
Emergency Repairs take 1 more Full Action. 
30% Only one weapon can be fired per Turn. Emergency 
Repairs take 2 more Full Actions. 
15% Can only fire weapons OR move in a Turn. Emergency  
Repairs heal half the Breakpoints. 
-- Unmanned ship, all systems offline. 
 
MANPOWER AND PASSENGERS 
Passengers of a Ship are based on the Manpower of the Ship, unless 
specified otherwise. The total Passengers that can stay on a ship 
without modification is quadruple the total Manpower of a Ship. This 
means that for every 500 Crew, another 2,000 passenger s can safely 
be on the Ship. Ships may also specify their own Passenger count. 
 
DEFEAT EXPERIENCE (EXPERIENCE) 
When a Ship is defeated in combat, the  Experience listed is paid out 
to those involved in the combat. Defeat Experience is divided by all 
Characters who took part in Combat , including NPCs . Defeat 
Experience should never account for pilots or Characters that are 
within the defeated Ship, as these Ships take hundreds of personnel 
to crew. 
 
ACCELERATION 
Unlike Vehicles, Ship Acceleration is the only speed -based 
Characteristic needed to track vehicle speeds outside of Slipspace. 
Accelerate is the Vehicle’s Meters per Turn (MpT) that they can 
increase their speed in a Full Action Turn.  The Ship can only move in 
the direction it is facing unless specified otherwise. 
 Ships do not have a Top Speed to track as there is no upper 
echelon of how fast a ship can go, given enough time and energy. 
 Ships do not have a Brake Characteristic to slow down. 
Instead, Ships will use half of their listed Accelerate Characteristic to 
find how much a Ship can slow down in a given Turn without using 
Tactics that could improve these Characteristics. 
 
CRUISING SPEED 
Cruising Speed is an available Movement Speed where the Ship can’t 
make any Attack Actions but can multiply their Accelerate by 10 as a 
Full Action. Cruising Speed allows ships to cover much more distance 
in a smaller amount of time. When a Ship uses Cruis ing Speed, the 
Ship will be at a -20 Penalty To Hit until the beginning of the Ship’s 
next Turn. When the Ship is using Cruising Speed, the Ship’s 
Maneuver is halved until the beginning of the Ship’s next Turn. 
 
EMERGENCY ACCELERATION 
A Ship is able to enter Emergency Acceleration as a Full Action. When 
Emergency Acceleration is activated, the Ship can’t use any other 
equipment or weapons until the Ship drops out of Emergency 
Acceleration as a Full Action. While active, the Ship will multiply their 
Accelerate by 25. While moving at this Speed, Maneuver Tests are 
impossible to make, however the Ship will be at a -30 Penalty to be 
Hit by Opponents. The Ship’s Turn Radius from the Maneuver 
Characteristic is halved. 
 
MANEUVER 
A Ship’s Maneuver is the limitation of a Pilot’s ability to use their 
Evasion Skill based on the vessel being piloted . If a Pilot has a total 
Evasion of 60, but the Ship has a Maneuver of 45, then the Pilot’s 
Evasion is capped at 45 while operating the vessel. 
 If an Attack is coming from the rear of the Ship, the Ship will 
have a -20 Penalty to the Evasion Test it is making. 
 The Ship’s Maneuver Characteristic, halved, is the amount 
of degrees the Ship can turn in a single Turn. 
 
CONCEAL 
A Ship’s Conceal Characteristic is the Ship’s capability to hide it’s 
footprint, allowing for them to stay hidden from sensors for a longer 
period of time. A Ship’s Conceal Characteristic is used to Oppose 
another Ship’s Sensor Characteristic. 
For every 100,000 Kilometers away, the Ship’s Conceal will 
gain a +10 Bonus. Instances and events of radiation, hiding behind 
celestial bodies, and more can be used to give further bonuses to 
Coneal. 
A Ship can use Passive Conceal to see how long it takes for 
them to be spotted, or continuously work to Conceal Itself as a Half 
Action. For every Degree of Success, the Ship’s active Conceal will last 
for 1 minute (10 Turns). This will allow Ships to acti vely monitor how 
well they’re attempting to hide themselves, without knowledge of the 
opposing Sensor Characteristics. The Character attempting to 
perform the active Conceal can gain the benefits of their Camouflage 
Skill Test. 
 
PASSIVE CONCEAL 
When a scenario first begins, the Ship will roll on its Ship Conceal. This 
will be the passive result needed for the ship to attempt to stay 
hidden passively. The Ship will be spotted by any Passive Sensor that 
has more Degrees of Success or less Degrees of Failure. 
 
EVENT CONCEAL BONUS 
Hiding behind asteroid half the size of ship +5 
Hiding behind asteroid same size or larger +10 
Hiding behind a massive moon or planetoid +20 
Radiation between the ships +30 
Hiding on the other side of a planet +40 
Using Stealth Cloaking technology +60 
 
SENSOR 
A Ship’s Sensor Characteristic is the Ship’s ability to passively spot 
other Ships and vehicles by Opposing their Conceal Characteristic. The 
Ship’s Sensor is used the same way that Conceal is used. There is an 
active use and passive use of this Characteristic. 
 A Ship can use Passive Sensor to see how long it takes for 
them to spot a possible enemy ship, or Active Sensor to actively 
search for opposing Ships as a Half Action. For every Degree of 
Success, the Ship’s active Sensor will last for 1 minute (10 Turns). This 
will allow the Ship to actively monitor for other Ships that may be 
attempting to conceal themselves or have not been spotted, yet. The 
Character attempting to perform the active Sensor Test can gain the 
benefits of their Investigation Skill Test.

PASSIVE SENSOR 
When a scenario first begins, the Ship will roll on its Ship Sensor. This 
will be the passive result needed for a Ship’s Concealment to beat.  A 
Ship’s Passive Sensor having more Degrees of Success, or less Degrees

PHYSICAL ATTRIBUTES AND SHIP SIZE 
The Physical Attributes of a Ship are the Ship’s Length, Width, Height, 
Weight, and Ship Size. A Ship’s Size is determined using complex 
math. So in order to make it easier on you, the table below will show 
you Ship Size based on the Length, Width, and Height added together. 
This Characteristic is used for things like finding the speed of a Ship in 
Slipspace. 
 Ship Size is also a modifier in how easy your ship is to strike. 
In combat, Characters gain a +5 Bonus To Hit for every Ship Size larger 
the Target is than them, to a maximum of +30. Characters also gain a 
-5 Penalty To Hit for every Ship Size smaller the  Target is than them, 
to a maximum of -30. 
 Ship Weight varies heavily between the various types of 
Ships. Ships will use Tonnes (1,000 kg), kilotons (1,000 Tonnes), 
Megatons (1,000,000 Tonnes), and Gigatons (1,000,000,000 Tonnes).

ARMOR AND HIT LOCATION 
Similar to Vehicles, Ships also direction-based Armor. You will use the 
Hit Location chart below to find which Component is struck.  The 
Armor Locations are Front, Back, Side, Top, and Bottom. 
  A Vehicle’s Armor is how much Damage is ignored from 
each Attack on a Vehicle’s Breakpoints, before Pierce is figured in. 
 When Attacking a Ship, the Hit Location Chart will show 
what was struck, similar to Character Hit Locations. If a Hit Location 
was struck that the Ship does not have or was destroyed, it will hit the 
Hull. There are no To Hit Penalties for making Called Shots to a Ship’s 
Hull. Struck locations will have their Breakpoints reduced if Damage 
is dealt.

BREAKPOINTS 
Ships are made up of multiple components that act as thresholds for 
each part of the Ship; the Weaponry (WEP), mobility system (MOB), 
Engine (ENG), Optics (OP), and Hull (HUL). 
 When one of these Breakpoints reaches 0, that component 
is destroyed or rendered useless. If a vehicle has multiple of the same 
component, it is up to the GM or Players to track which is struck, as 
they will not share Breakpoints unless they are clustered together. 
 
SHIP WEAPONS 
Ships will use their own special Ship Weapons instead of using 
weapons from the main Mythic handbook. These Ship Weapons will 
have their own special Characteristics and rules dependent on what 
is used and how many are used at a time. Ship Weapons will be f ired 
using the Warfare Range Characteristic of those using the weapon. 
 If a Ship Weapon shares the same name as a Weapon in the 
Mythic handbook, you will use the Ship Weapon version found in this 
expansion. 
 Characters and Vehicles struck by Ship Weapons will be 
instantly killed. There are some exceptions to this rule, however. An 
example could include a single 20mm Point Defense Autocannon 
firing against a Character, instead of the whole battery of Point 
Defense Autocannons. the GM could use a 20mm weapon from the 
handbook and use that, instead of the Ship Weapon. 
 
WARSHIP ENERGY SHIELDS 
Ship Energy Shields follow the same rules as normal Energy Shields 
with one major exception. When a Warship has Energy Shields, the 
Energy Shield will also have the Warship Special Rule when figuring 
Damage against Energy Shields. 
 While Energy Shields are active on a Warship, the Energy 
Shields and the Warship are immune to Blast, Kill, Penetrating, and 
Kinetic Special Rules when fired from non-Ship Weapons. If struck by 
a Ship Weapon, the Energy Shields will only be immune to Kill.  
 
SLIPSPACE TRAVEL 
Vehicles that have a Slipspace Drive are the only Vehicles that can use 
Slipspace Travel. A Slipspace-capable flying vessel can enter Slipspace 
to cover vast distances based on its Ship Size. 
There are two types of Slipspace Travel, Intersystem and 
Interstellar. Intersystem Slipspace Travel allows a vessel to travel 
175,000 Kilometers per Ship Size of the Vehicle, to a maximum of 8 
Ship Size. Interstellar Slipspace Travel allows a Vehicle to travel 2.6 
Lightyears a day. If a Vehicle is smaller than 2 Ship Size, this is reduced 
to 0.5 Lightyears a day. Covenant vessels multiply the distances by 10, 
while Forerunner vessels multiply them by 20. Smaller vessels and 
vehicles can piggyback off of lar ger ships when they enter Slipspace, 
allowing them to travel at their speeds.  
It takes 7 Full Actions to enter Slipspace and 4 Full Actions 
to exit Slipspace. 
 Due to the nature of Slipspace, real Space and Slipspace do 
not align. The GM must roll a D100. On the following table will show 
how much shorter or longer the distances may be. A Player may then 
roll a Navigation (Slipspace) Test. For every Degree of Suc ces will 
reduce the distance needed to travel by 1 Lightyear, to a maximum of 
5. 
 Entering Slipspace while in atmosphere creates an 
explosion with Blast (X), Kill (Y), and deals 10D10+15, Pierce of 100. 
(X) is equal to the Ship Size, and (Y) is the Ship Size divided by 4. This 
does not damage the vehicles entering Slipspace and will detonate 
when the Slipspace portal closes. 
Exiting Slipspace will Scatter a ship 4D100 Kilometers. This 
is reduced by 10 Kilometers per Degrees of Success on a Navigation 
(Slipspace) Test. Covenant ships halve the final Scatter and 
Forerunner Ships cannot Scatter.

BLIND SLIPSPACE JUMP 
A Blind Slipspace Jump is where the Ship makes an emergency jump 
into Slipspace without taking the time to calculate a location. This is a 
desperation jump that will scatter the Ship 10D100 *1,000 Kilometers 
in a random direction.  This will allow a Ship to escape as quickly as 
possible while in a bad situation.  A Blind Jump cannot benefit from 
anything that would reduce the Scatter, including Guidance Beacons 
and Ship Components. 
When a Ship exits from a Blind Slipspace Jump, the Ship’s 
engines must cool down before another jump can be made due to this 
being very taxing on the Ship. The cooldown period is 3 minutes (30 
Turns). 
 To perform a Blind Slipspace Jump, the Ship will enter 
Slipspace at half of the time it originally would, to a minimum of 1 Half 
Action. 
 
SLIPSPACE GUIDANCE BEACONS 
Slipspace Guidance Beacons are navigational aid devices that can be 
placed on vessels, planets, and docking stations. When traveling to a 
Guidance Beacon, the Ship will halve the Scatter from leaving 
Slipspace. The cost of one is 4,000 cR and they weigh 851 KG.

BALANCING SHIP COMBAT SCENARIOS 
Ship combat has t hree basic rules to follow as a GM to attempt 
balancing more fair combat scenarios: Type, Price, and 3:1. 
 
BALANCE: TYPE AND PRICE 
Ship Types help create the first general idea of how strong a Ship 
might be. Corvettes are considered the weakest and are the least 
armed and armored of any type of Ship. From there, it is safe to 
assume that it takes a minimum of 2 Ships to safely combat a Ship of 
the next Type. Two Corvettes to combat a Frigate, two Frigates to 
combat a Destroyer, etc. 
 In 100DOS, the price of any weapon, vehicle, or ship is 
determined by its damage output, defensive capabilities, and any 
Special Rule or capability it has. This means that the higher the price, 
the stronger and more combat-viable a Ship is. If a Ship costs 500,000 
cR, it’s safe to assume you will need roughly 500,000 cR worth of Ship 
to combat it. This is a closer rule of thumb compared to Ship Types. 
 
BALANCE: 3:1 DOCTRINE 
The 3:1 Doctrine is how many Ships the UNSC needs against a 
Covenant Ship of the same Type  and expect a victory. This is due to 
Covenant Ships having nearly double the Hull  and its use of Energy 
Shields. 
 When a Covenant Ship does not have Energy Shields, it is 
expected that the UNSC still needs 2 ships for each same type 
Covenant Ship. However, nothing beats a well-placed MAC round. 
 
BATTLE GROUPS 
Battle Groups are organizations of Ships that range much smaller than 
a Fleet. Battle Groups that range from 3 to 15 Ships of varying types. 
There are many instances of Battle Groups ranging into the hundreds, 
but this is rare.  A Battle Group will have a name, with each ship 
involved being given a numbered callsign. 
Fleets range in the hundreds to thousands  of ships , each 
having refill stations, detection complexes, command HQs, and much 
more. These can be used as set pieces and roleplaying options for 
backup, retrofitting, and restocking ships. 
   
 
SHIP DAMAGE RULES 
 
BREAKPOINTS: HULL 
Hull is the body  and frame of a Ship. If the Hull Breakpoints are 
reduced to 0, the Ship’s Armor is halved on all Locations and life 
support will fail. Ships that have the Warship Special Rule will lose this 
Special Rule. The Ship then enters Doomed State. 
If an Attack strikes the Hull and has more Damage or Pierce 
than the vehicle has Armor, it will pierce through and deal its leftover 
Damage to the Ship’s Manpower. Weapons with Blast and Kill Radius 
will double the damage to Manpower. 
 
BREAKPOINTS: WEAPON 
When a weapon is struck on the To Hit Table, the GM gets to decide 
which weapon is struck. It is best for the GM to decide based on 
where the Ship was struck, choosing the closest weapon.  A Weapon 
that hits 0 Breakpoints cannot be fired and becomes useless or 
removed from the vehicle until repaired. 
 On Heavy and Medium Weapons, these Weapons are struck 
on a per-weapon basis. On Point Defense and Missile Weapons, these 
are struck on Port and Starboard sides. 
 
BREAKPOINTS: MOBILITY 
There are many types of Mobility Systems for Ships. All Ships are 
considered to have 4 Mobility drives that allow them to move. When 
a Mobility System reaches 0 Breakpoints, it is no longer usable to the 
vehicle. Each time a Mobility System is destroyed, the ship loses a 
quarter of its total Accelerate Characteristic. 
 
BREAKPOINTS: ENGINE 
When an Attack strikes the Engine, the Engine Breakpoints are 
reduced. When reduced to 0, the Ship can no longer power itself and 
will change to Emergency Power. 
 When a Ship is on Emergency Power, only Life Support, 
Weapons, and Mobility are active at low levels. Heavy Weapons and 
Energy Shields can’t be used. The Ship will have enough Emergency 
Power to last 6 hours. 
 When the Engine loses 25 or more Breakpoints in a Round, 
it is disabled for 1 Turn. A disabled Engine means the Ship cannot 
Accelerate or Maneuver for that Turn. 
  
BREAKPOINTS: OPTIC 
Optics are how a Ship is capable of seeing out into space. On Covenant 
Ships, these are camera systems. On UNSC ships, these are giant 
observatory domes and cockpits with hardened glass-like material. 
 When camera-based Optics are destroyed, they no longer 
see outside. Ships will have to depend on Sensors and other vehicles 
or Ships to relay information to Ships, giving a -20 Penalty To Hit on 
weapons. 
 When Glass-based Optics are destroyed, those within the 
cockpit will be in serious danger as they have no protection from both 
these Attacks and space, itself.

SHIP DOOMED STATE 
 
ENTERING A DOOMED STATE 
A Ship that has had its Hull reduced to 0 means the Ship no longer 
gains the benefits of Heavy Plating Special Rule, as well as a reduction 
of half of its Armor on all Locations. 
 
TAKING DAMAGE WHILE IN DOOMED STATE 
When a Ship takes Damage while in Doomed State, it begins taking 
adverse effects based on the Damage received afterwards. Below, 
you’ll find the table that shows the various effects based on the total 
Damage the Vehicle just took from each individual Attack. 
 
DAMAGE OUTCOME 
1-10 Nothing happens. 
11-20 Current Armor is reduced by 2 on all Locations. 
21-30 Current Armor is reduced by 3 on all Locations. Manpower 
is reduced by 1D10. 
31-40 Current Armor is reduced by 3 on all Locations. Manpower 
is reduced by 3D10. 
41-50 
Current Armor is reduced by 4 on all Locations. The Ship is 
left unable to move or Evade until the beginning of the 
Attacker’s next Turn. Manpower is reduced by 3D10. 
51-65 
Current Armor is reduced by 5 on all Locations. Sections of 
the Ship catch fire that are still sealed from space. All 
Characters in those spaces take Flame (3) each Round until 
they escape. Manpower is reduced by 5D10. 
66-80 
Current Armor is reduced by 10 on all Locations. A GM -
chosen location of import of the Ship is destroyed. The 
Ship’s ammunition and fuel storage will begin to detonate. 
The Characters have 5 minutes (50 Turns) to escape the 
vehicle before it detonates. The Blast Radius is the Ship Size 
Points multiplied by 100. The Kill Radius is half of the Blast 
Radius. 
81-99 
The Ship violently explodes due to damage to the fuel and 
ammunition systems. This gives the Characters only 3 
minutes (30 Turns) to escape before full detonation. The 
Blast Radius is the Ship Size Points multiplied by 125. The Kill 
Radius is half of the Blast Radius. 
100+ 
The Ship instantly detonates, killing everyone inside. The 
Blast Radius is the Ship Size Points multiplied by 150. The Kill 
Radius is half of the Blast Radius. 
 
ESCAPING A DOOMED SHIP 
To escape, the Characters must rush to escape pods and drop pod 
vehicles that will allow them to leave the ship before the time is up. 
It will take 2 minutes, on average, for a Character with an Agility of 30 
to make it to a location that would allow them to escape. 
 
SHIP DETONATION 
When a Ship detonates while in Doomed State, it deals Damage based 
on its Ship Size Points. For every Ship Size, the Ship detonates dealing 
1D10+5 Damage. This Damage is doubled against Characters and 
Vehicles that do not have the Warship Special Rule. 
 
SHIPS IN ATMOSPHERE AND CRASH LANDING 
Ships that are not rated to be inside the Atmosphere of a planet must 
have External Atmospheric Thrusters installed before entry. These 
Thrusters stop the Ship from crash-landing. When a Ship  Crash Lands, 
the Ship will take 4D10*10 Damage. Each 10 rolled will cause another 
1D10 Damage to be rolled. The total rolled is then multiplied again by 
the vessel’s Ship Size. This Damage ignores Armor and is deals the 
Damage directly to the Hull. 
 
 
SHIP SPECIAL DAMAGE 
Ships can take Special Damage, similar to a Character. Warships can 
only take Special Damage from Ship Weapons, MAC Cannons, and 
Vehicle-based Energy Weapons like a Scarab’s beam, the Zeus Cannon, 
etc. The GM should use discretion when deciding what will work. 
 Ships will take Special Damage the same way that normal 
Characters would take Special Damage, by Damage Dice rolling a 
natural 10 when figuring Damage.

SHIP REPAIRS 
Ships can be Repaired using the Technology Characteristic. When Ship 
Repairs are needed, especially in larger and larger vessels, more 
Characters who are capable of assisting and repairing are needed. 
When Manpower begins to get too low, the rate and amount repaired 
will begin to suffer. There are two types of Ship Repairs, Emergency 
and Corrective. 
 
EMERGENCY REPAIRS 
Emergency Repairs take very little time to perform but the amount of 
Emergency Repairs that can be performed on a Ship is limited. 
Emergency Repairs are fast, temporary measures to keep a Ship 
active while in combat. 
 When making an Emergency Repair, the Crew will When 
doing Emergency Repairs, there will be a lead Maintenance Specialist. 
This Character will be the main Characteristic used to do the repair. 
When making a Repair Test, keep in mind that it will take 5 Ful l 
Actions to perform, which could take even longer if the crew will need 
to travel to the location, or if there are things that could block their 
ability to get to where they need to begin repairs.  
When a Repair Test is made, it will repair 5D10 in 
Breakpoints. For every Degree of Success, the Test will repair an extra 
20 Breakpoints, to a maximum of 5 Degrees of Success.  
 Each time an Emergency Repair is made, the amount 
repaired is reduced by 1D10 and the maximum Degrees of Success is 
reduced by 1. Once the D10s rolled to repair the ship have been 
reduced to 0, the Ship can no longer take Emergency Repairs until a 
Corrective Repair is performed. 
 
CORRECTIVE REPAIRS 
Corrective Repairs are long -term Repairs that can’t be done by a 
standard ship crew with tools that would be found on a ship. 
Corrective Repairs take docking stations and special repair crews to 
perform. 
 When undergoing Corrective Repairs, it will repair all of the 
Ship’s Breakpoints. For every 10 Breakpoint repaired, it will take one 
hour. 
 In the occurrence that the Characters would be charged for 
Corrective Repairs, it will cost 1 cR for every 10 Breakpoints repaired. 
 
RECOVERING MANPOWER AND WAGES 
Recovering Manpower can be done in two ways. The first way is the 
Military way. Between missions, the Manpower of a Ship will be 
automatically restored when at docking stations or military stations.  
In non -military settings, Manpower would have to be 
restored by paying wages. To recover Manpower with Wages, it 
would cost 50 cR for every missing Manpower to be restored. If the 
GM wants, they could charge this monthly with the idea that the ship 
runner would need to pay at least some of how the Manpower 
sustains their way of life. 
 
SHIP WEAPONS 
Ship Weapons are placed into groups of Heavy, Medium, Point, and 
Missile. Each Weapon Type will have its own way of firing or rules for 
how you use them. 
 
HEAVY WEAPONS: 
Heavy Weapons are grouped into large -scale weapons like MAC 
Cannons and Energy Projectors. These weapons strike instantly and 
deal large sets of Damage. 
 Heavy Weapons are all fired separately with their own To 
Hit Test and Damage. Heavy Weapons can only target Ships directly 
ahead in the Fore, found as the red line on the Ship Facing image. 
 When a Heavy Weapon rolls a 10 on the Damage Dice, you 
will roll another 1D10. You will do this each time a 10 is rolled. 
MEDIUM WEAPONS: 
Medium weapons are categorized into heavier -hitting weapons that 
are each fired separately with their own Attack Action.  
Medium Weapons are stationed evenly on the Port and 
Starboard of the Ship. If there is only one, it is stationed at the bottom 
or top of the ship, capable of hitting in all directions.  If there is an 
uneven amount of a Medium Weapon, one will be stationed at the 
bottom or top of the ship while the rest are stationed evenly on the 
Port and Starboard of the ship. 
Medium Weapons are all fired separately with their own To 
Hit Test and Damage. 
 
POINT DEFENSE WEAPONS: 
Point Defense Weapons are large groups of weapon batteries. Point 
Defense Weapons are split into two groups, Port and Starboard, each 
holding half of the Point Defense Weapons. This means that the Point 
Defense Weapons on the Port side wouldn’t be able to make Attacks 
against Targets on the Starboard side.  
Point Defense weapons can be used to split up Attacks 
against multiple Targets. Point Defense weapons are fired as a whole 
at an intended Target, though you can also separate these weapons 
between Targets. If you have 10 Port Rampart weapons, you will fire  
all 10 of them at your Target at once, with a single To Hit Test. For 
every 10 of them firing, you get a +5 To Hit.  
When finding Damage, you will roll the Damage listed on 
the Weapon’s table and multiply the Base Damage by the amount 
that are firing. 
 
MISSILE WEAPONS: 
Missiles are groups of missile launchers that will attack with explosive 
volume. Missiles take time to travel and will detonate against its 
Target. Missiles will take 1 Turn to impact its Target for every 10 
kilometers it has to travel. 
 Missiles listed are the amount of launchers. Each Launcher 
will hold 10 total missiles that can be fired. This means if a Ship lists 
180 Archer Missiles, they will have 1,800 total missiles that can be 
fired, but only 180 can be fired in a Turn. 
 Missile weapons can be used to split up Attacks against 
multiple Targets. Missile weapons are fired as a whole at intended 
Targets, letting the Player decide how many will be fired and how 
many of each go after each Target. For every 10 missiles that were  
fired, you gain a +5 To Hit. 
 When finding Damage, you will roll the Damage listed on 
the Weapon’s table and multiply the Base Damage by the amount 
that were fired. 
 Nuclear Missiles will be fired separately and not in bursts or 
swarms. Attempting to use Point Defense to stop these missiles are 
at a -40 Penalty To Hit due to their velocity and small size. 
 
BOMBER WEAPONS: 
Bombing Weapons won’t be listed under Weapons on a Ship’s list, but 
instead as the Orbital Bombardment Special Rule. Bombing Weapons 
are designed to target and strike down at ground -level targets on 
nearby planets. These can’t be used against mobile, working space 
vessels. 
 
SHIP AUTOLOADERS 
All Ships make use of Autoloaders for their weapons. The listed 
Reload Time of all Ship Weapons is the amount of Half Actions it takes 
for them to Reload using the Autoloader.  
 Only Point Defense weapons can be Reloaded without an 
Autoloader, which is a process that takes 2 Manpower per weapon to 
reload them.

WARSHIP SPECIAL RULE 
Ships with the Warship Special Rule can’t be damaged by infantry 
weapons. Ship Weapons, Coilguns, and Railguns will deal full Damage 
unless they are infantry weapons. The following Vehicle Weapons will 
be able to deal 1/4th of their total Damage and Pierce to Warships: 
• Autocannons of 20mm or larger 
• Cannons 
• Energy Weapons 
The GM may decide additional weapons to deal 1/4 th Damage to 
Ships, though these should be done sparingly as there should be no 
possibility of infantry weapons destroying a Warship. The GM may 
decide that a very small Warship of Ship Size 1 or 2 could be taken out 
by a Spartan Laser, as an example of an outlier happening. 
 
DROPSHIPS AND FIGHTERS 
When dealing with Dropships and Fighters, it’s important to 
understand that these are to be considered Vehicles and not Ships. 
This means that they will do less Damage to Ships and are at a much 
higher chance of being destroyed by Ships in a single Turn. 
 
TACTICS 
Tactics are Full Actions that allow Ships to perform special skills. Only 
one Tactic can be performed during a Turn and some can be 
prolonged to last more than a single Turn if desired. 
 
EVASIVE MANEUVERS 
Evasive Maneuvers is a Full Action where the Ship’s heading is 
drastically changed in both speed and direction. When using Evasive 
Maneuvers, the Ship will gain a +20 Bonus to Maneuver and a -20 
Penalty to any To Hit Tests made. This lasts until the beginning of the 
Ship’s next Turn. 
 
MISSILE DEFENSE 
The Ship will redirect Point Defense Weapons to target and take down 
incoming Missiles. When doing this, the Point Defense Weapons 
being used won’t be able to make Attacks against Targets until the 
beginning of the next Turn. The Point Defense Weapons can only hit 
Missiles at half of the Weapon’s Optimal Range and can’t destroy the 
missiles beyond this Range. Missile Defense can also use its Missile 
Launchers to do the same Action, but the missile’s travel time still 
must be taken into account. 
 The person behind using these weapons will make a 
Warfare Range Test. If Successful, a missile will be destroyed for every 
two Point Defense weapons being used. For every Degree of Success, 
each Point Defense weapon will shoot down one more Missile. 
 
HARD TURN 
A Hard Turn has the Ship rotating as heavily in a chosen direction as 
possible to attempt to slow itself down. As a Full Action, the Ship 
Turns beyond its normal allotted safety measures.  The Ship will turn 
facing equal to its Maneuver Characteristic mult iplied by 2, to a 
maximum of 180 degrees. This will slow down the Ship equal to the 
Maneuver Characteristic multiplied by 10, to a minimum of 0. 
 When a Hard Turn is made, the Ship will gain a -20 Penalty 
To Hit on all Attacks made until the beginning of the Ship’s next Turn. 
 
RAM 
A Ram is where a Ship is directed to crash into a Target. Ram Actions 
are at a -(X) Penalty to Evade using Maneuvers, where (X) is 5 per each 
Size Point of the Ship. 
 Ramming deals Damage based on the Ship’s Characteristics 
and current speed being traveled. The Ship will deal (X)D100 Damage, 
where (X) is equal to the Ship’s Size Points. Each D100 rolled cannot 
roll under 50 Damage. 
 The Pierce of the Ram Action is +1 for every 500 KM the Ship 
is traveling, to a maximum of 30 Pierce. Ram Actions have the Kinetic 
Special Rule. 
 The Ramming Ship will take Half of the Damage it dealt. 
Ramming will deal Damage against Energy Shields before it will 
Damage the Hull. If the Ramming Ship only strikes Energy Shields and 
never damages the Target’s Hull, the Ship will take only a quarter of 
the Damage. 
 
SLINGSHOT 
When a Ship is in Cruising Speed or Emergency Acceleration, they are 
able to use the gravitational pull of a celestial body to wrap around. 
When doing so, this Acceleration is doubled per Turn.  
While a Slingshot is being performed, the Ship will quickly 
drag towards the Celestial body, needing a -20 Pilot Test per Turn. If 
Successful, the Ship will continue its path safely. If Failed, the Ship will 
begin to enter Atmosphere and lose all of its ex tra momentum from 
the Slingshot. If Failed with 2 or more Degrees of Failure, the Ship will 
be dragged into the atmosphere and be forced out of Cruising Speed 
and Emergency Acceleration. If Failed with 3 or more Degrees of 
Failure, the Ship will be deeply dragged into the Atmosphere and 
begin to descend to ground level at a rapid pace. 
If the celestial body has no atmosphere, the Ship won’t be 
deeply dragged towards the surface of the Planet unless the Test has 
4 or more Degrees of Failure. 
 
MANNED STATIONS 
Manned Stations are locations of a Ship that can be controlled by a 
Character. Manned Positions are the Positions within that Station. 
This allows  there to be player involvement with the Ship.  Manned 
Stations are based on the locations of the Ship, its Weapons, and 
more. The following section will list Manned Stations, both necessary 
and optional. 
 
STATION: MAIN DECK 
The Main Deck is where the Captain, pilots, and weapon users of the 
Ship will be present. This is a location that changes vastly based on 
the type of Ship is being used. Smaller Ships will have a small room for 
this, generally with only two seats for a Pilot and a Co -Pilot. Larger 
Ships will have entire decks with enough seating for everyone 
necessary to pilot a Ship.

MAIN DECK POSITION: CAPTAIN 
The Captain is a leadership-based Manned Position that exists to give 
commands and keep everything in order. On small civilian ships like 
on Shuttles and Transports, the Captain doubles as the Pilot , which 
offers no benefits whatsoever besides knowing who is in charge. 
 On Warships, the Captain will give commands to keep 
everyone working together. This position will not have the Captain 
physically manning the Ship, but instead will allow a Character to 
make Leadership Tests once per Round to a given Position on the Deck 
to perform a commanded action. 
 If the Test is Successful, that commanded Character will 
gain a +5 to the commanded Test, with an extra +5 for every Degree 
of Success, to a maximum of +20. 
 On Small Ships, if the Captain is also Piloting, the Captain 
will not be able to make a Leadership Test and a Pilot Test during the 
same Turn due to this being two separate Positions on one Station. 
 
MAIN DECK POSITION: PILOT 
The Pilot position, when not being used by the Captain on smaller 
Ships, is for a Character to control the main movements of the Ship. 
This is a necessary Position that must be filled. This involves plotting 
courses, using Maneuver to make Evasions, and more. The Pilot will 
use their Pilot (Space) Skill while in this Position. 
 
MAIN DECK POSITION: CO-PILOT (OPTIONAL) 
The Co-Pilot position is a Position that can be used on any Ship, even 
smaller Ships. Even though this Position is available on any Ship, it is 
also optional. For example, the Co -Pilot position is also a gunner 
position on smaller Ships, such as a Transport vessel. A Character in a 
Station that could be two separate Positions, only one may be used 
during a Turn. 
 When a Co-Pilot is assisting a Pilot, they are able to make a 
Pilot (Space) Skill Test. If Successful, the Pilot is given a +5 Bonus to 
their Test for various Tests being made, such as Maneuvers or Tactics. 
For every Degree of Success, the Pilot gains an extra +5 Bonus, to a 
maximum of +20. 
 This Bonus can’t stack with a Captain giving commands to 
the Pilot. 
MAIN DECK POSITION: AI COMPUTER (OPTIONAL) 
The AI Computer can only be taken if the AI Computer  System 
Component was purchased for the Ship.  This allows a Smart AI or 
Dumb AI to have access to the Ship ’s systems, allowing them to man 
up to two total Positions per Turn. For example, the Smart AI aboard 
the Ship could both Pilot the Ship and man a set of Weapons. An AI 
cannot support itself in roles, such as being its own Co-Pilot. 
 
STATION: WEAPON 
The Weapon Station is a Station designed to house all of those who 
are in charge of firing weapons on a Ship. There are multiple layouts 
of a Weapon Station,  such as being its own location on a Ship, built 
into the Main Deck, or even being based at the location of the 
Weapons on the Ship. 
A Weapon Position is a Manned Position  on the Weapon 
Station that controls an entire Array of Weapons at once. For example, 
a single Character can be set in charge of the entire array of Ares 
Missile Pods aboard a Ship.  Weapon Positions may also be split 
among multiple Characters, such as two Characters having the Ares 
Missile Pods of the Ship split between them.  
There are certain types of Ship Weapons that must be split 
between two positions, which are P oint Defense W eapons. Point 
Defense Weapons are split between Port and Starboard positions, 
meaning a Character will control the entire Port side of Weapons on 
a ship, while the second Character will control the weapons on the 
Starboard side. In a small Ship, such as a Civilian Transport, a single 
Character would have to use both of these “Positions” under the 
same Station, meaning that the Character would only be a ble to fire 
the Port side of weapons instead of the Starboard side. 
For Heavy and Medium Weapons, each named set of 
weapons can be linked and given to a single Character to man, or each 
can be given to a Character to be manned separately. There are some 
examples where this is not possible, such as a Ship having multiple 
MAC Cannons, which can only fire directly forward, meaning only one 
Character would be able to properly make use of this weapon at a 
time. 
 
OPTIONAL BAY STATIONS 
Each and every Bay can be considered a Station. Security Bays need 
someone to run Security and to make sure people are safe. Armory 
Stations need people to tend to equipment and acquisition. Cargo 
Holds need people keeping stock of items. The Players and the GM 
should always work together on these Stations. They can be used for 
roleplaying, gaining benefits and bonuses to their desired Actions, 
and much more. The larger the Ship, the more benefits should be 
given by these Bays and their capability as a Station. 
 
PREMADE WARSHIPS 
Premade Warships are Warship Frames that have all the basic needs 
based on various Warship Frames. These are found in the UNSC 
Warships (p. 15) and Covenant Warship (p. 27) sections of the 
handbook. 
 All Premade Warships come  with the basic needs of a 
Warship, including listed Ship Weapons (p. 11) and Ship Special Rules 
(p. 14). A Premade Warship should be considered to have all needed 
Components for that Ship to run, while some Ship Special Rules will 
list other Components that are installed. 
 All Premade Warships are considered to have the following 
Components, which can be found in the What Comes With a Frame 
(p. 38) section of the Handbook.  The GM may also decide whether a 
Ship will come with more Components , though each Premade 
Warship is to be considered built with only the most basic 
Components needed to fly. 
 
• Standard Armor 
• Standard Drive 
• Standard Computing System 
• Standard Crew Quarters 
• Hangar Bay 
• Cafeteria 
• Docking Bay 
• Armory 
• Airlock Chamber 
• Escape Pods (1 per Ship Size) 
 
RETROFITTING AND MODIFYING SHIPS

Component, the Building and Modifying Ships  (p. 37) section of the

SHIP SPECIAL RULES 
All Ship Special Rules can be found with more information in the Ship Creation and Modification (p. 37) section of the handbook. 
 
SHIP SPECIAL RULES DESCRIPTION 
Cloaking System 
Capable of rendering the ship completely invisible, giving it a + 50 to Conceal while active. The Cloaking System can stay active 
for 12 Turns before recharging for 20 Turns. While active, if a Ship is sensed by another, it will still be at a -30 Penalty to be struck 
by Attacks that are not Missile Weapons. 
EW Suite Blocks all communication between opposing Ships within 10 Kilometers of this Ship. The communication will also be blocked if 
this Ship is between the opposing Ship and the source they are trying to communicate with.  
Frigate Bay The Ship has a Frigate Bay which can be found in the Bay Components (p. 43). Premade Ships will list the amount of Frigates they 
hold by (X). 
Luminary The Luminary is a scanning device that is capable of scanning for Forerunner technology up to 385,000 kilometers from the Ship. 
This is a process that takes 10 minutes. 
Orbital 
Bombardment 
Orbital Bombardment allows for the Ship to make use of Strike Ordnance and Light Ordnance. These are launched from the Ship 
as a Full Action. 
Hyperscanner The Hyperscanner gives the Ship a +10 to Passive Sensor and +30 to Sensor Tests. 
Stealth Shroud The Stealth Shroud gives the Ship a +10 to Passive Conceal and +30 to Conceal Tests. 
Slipspace Specialist The Ship is able to enter and exit Slipspace as a Full Action. 
Long Range This allows the Ship to double the Optimal and Longe Range of all weapons. 
Slipspace Probe 
A Slipspace Probe Launcher that will send out Probes as a Full Action. Once active, these Probes will scan the surrounding 
equivalent locations in Slipspace to watch for Ships. When another vessel is going to exit Slipspace within 100,000 KM of the  
Ship, the Ship will be warned the Turn prior. 
Rear Deck The Ship’s Deck is located at the rear of the Ship, instead of the front or top. This means that only Attacks from the Rear c an 
strike the Optic Breakpoint Location. 
Atmospheric Entry The Ship can safely enter and exit the atmosphere of a planet without any issue or the need of specialized external thrusters. 
Hex-Octagonal Hull The Ship is given +100 Breakpoints to the Hull.

BUILDING AND MODIFYING SPACESHIPS 
 
In this section, you will be able to build your own space-faring vessels 
based on modular choices. These choices can also be used to modify 
and expand existing ships found in Mythic Space. Nonetheless, this 
section of the handbook is designed to allow you to build these 
vessels both within and outside of the Halo universe. Keep this in 
mind when you use this section to build a spaceship, as these building 
blocks were not what was used to create the UNSC and Covenant 
warships in Mythic Space. These use a com pletely separate system 
that will come out to different pricing and systems overall. 
 When building a ship, keep in mind that these 
Characteristics will be for human -based ships. The GM may modify 
the Characteristics where needed to meet their needs. 
 
SHIP FRAME 
The Ship’s Frame is the first building block needed when making a 
ship. The frame will decide the base Ship Size, Breakpoints, and other 
base Characteristics you’ll need to use a ship. 
 When selecting a Ship Frame, you’ll first have the option to 
select the accompanying Ship Size. Some Ship Frames will only have a 
single Ship Size, which means you won’t have the option to choose 
another. Ship Size is important, as it is the likelihood of  being struck 
while in Combat, but also increases the amount of systems and 
options you can place in your Ship as you build it out and modify it. 
  
CIVILIAN FRAMES 
All Civilian are considered a Vehicle, instead of a Ship. This means that 
the Armor and Hull that these vessels come with are too light to gain 
extra benefits against what would be considered smaller -form 
weaponry. There are upgrades that allow for a Civil ian Frame to gain 
the full defensive benefits of being a Ship while in Combat. 
 When finding Civilian Frame sizes, you may need to need 
the Vehicle Size, for whatever your needs may be. To find this, you 
add together the Length, Width, and Height and divide it by 6. In the 
Civilian Frames, you’ll see “Vehicle (Ship) Size. This will s how the 
minimum and maximum Vehicle Size, alongside what that equates to 
in Ship Size.

WARSHIP FRAMES 
All Warship Frames come with the Warship Special Rule. When 
selecting a Warship, Characters must either be in the Military or have 
black market or backend connections that would allow them access 
to something so powerful as a Warship Frame. 
 
FINDING SHIP FRAME WEIGHT 
The Ship Frame is the heaviest part of a ship, accounting for the vast 
majority of its total weight. To find a Ship’s Weight, add the Length, 
Width, and Height together and then divide it by 400. That will be 
your weight in Megatonnes. 
 
CONSTRUCTION TIME 
The Construction Time of a Ship depends on the Ship Size. For every 
Ship Size the vessel is, it will take 2 months of construction to 
complete the Frame. From there, certain options can increase the 
Construction Time needed. 
 
RETROFITS 
A Retrofit is when a Ship is brought in for modification. When a Ship 
is brought in for a Retrofit, it will take 1 week per Components and 1 
day per weapon, as well as a 100 cR per week charge on construction. 
 
COMPONENTS 
Components are building blocks a Ship can have installed. 
Components include Armor, Drives, Computers, Crew, and Bays.  The 
larger the Ship, the larger many Components will be. This only affects 
the Cost when the it lists per Ship Size. 
 
COMPONENT SLOTS 
Component Slots determine how many Components can be installed 
into a Ship. For every 1 Ship Size a Ship is, it will gain 4 Component 
Slots. If a Component does not list Component Slots, then it will take 
none to install.

Certain weapons have limitations on how many of each type of

SENSOR AND CONCEAL 
A Ship’s Censor and Conceal will be decided by the chosen Computer 
System Component. At default, the Standard Computing System will 
be installed if none are chosen. 
 
DEFEAT EXPERIENCE 
A custom Ship’s Defeat Experience is 1% of the total cost to create the 
Ship, to a maximum of 2,000 Experience. 
 
WHAT COMES WITH A FRAME 
When a Frame is first chosen, it will come with basic Components to 
fit the most basic needs of those on board. Ship Frames come with 
the following standard Components: 
 
FRAME ARMOR 
Standard, unmodified Armor. This is a combination of Armor Plating 
and Hull’s structural support. 
 
FRAME DRIVE 
All Ship Frames will come with the Tanith TKOFZ -2 Fusion Rockets. 
These are the cheapest and most manufactured Drives available. 
 
FUSION REACTOR 
Ships will come with a Fusion Reactor designed to power the Frame 
and its various components. 
 
COMPUTING SYSTEM 
The Ship will come installed with a Standard Computing System . This 
gives the Ship its Sensor and Conceal Characteristics. 
 
SHAW-FUJIKAWA TRANSLIGHT ENGINE 
All Ships will come with Shaw -Fujikawa Translight Engines that 
transition Ships in and out of Slipspace. 
 
STANDARD CREW QUARTERS 
All Ships will come with enough rooms to house the listed Manpower 
and Passengers. These will offer beds and storage lockers where 
multiple people live in communal spaces. 
 
STANDARD DECK 
A Standard Deck is an all -purpose control deck with computing and 
piloting systems that allow the Crew to use the vehicle.

Manpower and Passenger space to allow your crew the room needed 
to accommodate them.  
 
STEP 2: CHOOSING NEW COMPONENTS 
Each Frame comes with enough base Components to allow you to 
begin using the Ship right away. However, these Ships will offer no 
benefits outside of your standard small -scale personal storage and 
protection from space, itself. 
 In the section after the Civilian and Warship Frames, you 
will find a large assortment of Components that can be installed into 
a Ship before it leaves the manufacturing line. 
 
STEP 3: SELECTING WEAPONS 
After all is said and done and you have selected the frame and 
components desired, you can start selecting your Ship Weapons.  
 
INSTALLING WEAPONS ON CIVILIAN FRAMES 
When a Weapon is installed onto a Civilian Frame, it will have 125 
Breakpoints no matter the Frame that it was installed on. This is due 
to Civilian Frames not being designed to host weapons, causing them 
to be less overall protected.

of a Frigate or Destroyer in terms of weapon count. A Colony Frame

ARMOR COMPONENTS 
While Armor comes built into a Frame during its manufacturing 
process, there are ways to modify them both after the initial 
manufacturing process using refits, and during the manufacturing 
process under special order. 
 Below is a series of Armor Upgrades that can be taken to 
bolster any Ship’s capabilities, including a refitting that turns Civilian 
Vessels into full-blown Ships under standard classification. 
 
CIVILIAN CLASSIFICATION RETROFIT 
COST: -- cR 
The Civilian Classification Retrofit will reclassify the Civilian Vehicle as 
a Ship, giving it the capability to withstand much more under combat. 
This Retrofit will give the Vehicle much stronger Titanium -Alloy 
Plating that will allow it to withstand more  dangers that come with 
space travel, as well as Combat. This doubles the Ship’s weight. 
 
A-2 TITANIUM-ALLOY PLATING 
COST: 1,000 cR per Ship Size 
A-2 Titanium-Allow is an advanced Allow that is a sizeable upgrade 
from the original Titanium -A plating. This Plating will increase the 
Armor by +3 on all Locations. This can’t stack with A-3 Plating. 
 
A-3 TITANIUM-ALLOY PLATING 
COST: 3,000 cR per Ship Size 
A-3 Titanium-Allow is an advanced Allow that is a large upgrade from 
the original Titanium-A plating. This Plating will increase the Armor by 
+6 on all Locations. This can’t stack with A-2 Plating. 
 
THERMAL SUPERCONDUCTING RADIATOR 
COST: 3,300 cR 
Thermal Superconducting Radiators are sprawled across the Hull of 
the Ship allowing for it to more efficiently transfer heat across the 
body and back into space. When installed, the Ship is able to ignore 
the Cauterize Special Rule and halve all Damage from Flame and Fire. 
 
RE-F1 ARMOR REINFORCEMENT 
COST: 1,000 cR per Ship Size 
RE-F1 Armor Reinforcement is an additive layer of Titanium-A Plating. 
When installed, this will increase the weight of the Ship by 25% and 
give +3 Armor to all Locations. This is not compatible with any other 
RE-F Armor Reinforcement Upgrades. 
 
RE-F2 ARMOR REINFORCEMENT 
COST: 2,000 cR per Ship Size 
RE-F2 Armor Reinforcement is an additive layer of Titanium-A Plating. 
When installed, this will increase the weight of the Ship by 50% and 
give +6 Armor to all Locations. This is not compatible with any other 
RE-F Armor Reinforcement Upgrades. 
 
RE-F3 ARMOR REINFORCEMENT 
COST: 3,000 cR per Ship Size 
RE-F3 Armor Reinforcement is an additive layer of Titanium-A Plating. 
When installed, this will increase the weight of the Ship by 75% and 
give +9 Armor to all Locations. This is not compatible with any other 
RE-F Armor Reinforcement Upgrades. 
 
HEX-OCTAGONAL HULL SECTIONING 
COST: 1,500 cR per Ship Size 
The Hex-Octagonal Hull Sectioning is a specialized structural upgrade 
that gives the Ship’s Hull a way to better reduce damage caused from 
Attacks. This gives the Ship a +100 Breakpoints to Hull. 
 
DRIVE COMPONENTS 
A Drive is the way a vehicle propels and maneuvers its way through 
space. Maneuver Drives are the main drives used in spaceships. 
However, there are specialized drives that improve the function and 
capabilities of a ship. Only two Drive Upgrades can be installed into a 
Ship at a time, each taking up two of the mobility options found in the 
Breakpoints section of this handbook. The GM and Players must track 
the MOB Breakpoints of both of them separately, as if both of a Fusion 
Drive upgrade breaks, the benef its are lost alongside the drawbacks 
of being down on mobility propulsion. 
 
TANITH TKOFZ-2 FUSION ROCKETS

will come with 3 uses and each use is 100,000 cR to replace if 
destroyed. 
 If taken after the year 2554, the price of a Slipspace COM

SUPERLUMINAL COMMUNICATION ARRAY 
COST: 1,500,000 cR 
A Superluminal Communication Array is a specialized communication 
system that uses quantum entanglement to send information 
through both space and Slipspace. This allows for near instantaneous 
communication between devices that share the Superluminal 
Communication Array. The Superluminal Communication Array can 
also send information near instantaneously to tethered devices  that 
do not have this technology.  
 If taken after the year 255 8, the price of the Superluminal 
Communication Array is reduced by 50%.
